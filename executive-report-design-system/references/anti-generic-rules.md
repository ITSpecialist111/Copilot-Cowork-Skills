# Anti-Generic Rules

*On-demand reference for `executive-report-design-system`. Read this if a report looks
right mechanically but still reads as machine-generated.*

---

## The problem this solves

A language model left to its own devices produces a recognisable house style. Not a bad
style exactly — a *default* one. Everybody has now seen it enough times to recognise it
instantly, and recognition is the whole problem: the moment a reader clocks the format,
they assume nobody spent any time on the content either.

The tell is never one thing. It is the accumulation:

- a centred hero with a purple-to-blue gradient
- three or four identical cards, each with an emoji
- 16px `Inter`, generous uniform padding, everything the same weight
- 12–16px rounded corners and a soft drop shadow on every surface
- a heading called *Key Insights*, then exactly three bullets
- colour used decoratively on every element
- a footer admitting the thing was AI-generated

None of those is wrong on its own. Together they are a fingerprint.

---

## The bans

These are mechanical and non-negotiable. `scripts/check_output.py` enforces most of them.

### Visual

| Banned | Instead |
|---|---|
| Emoji, anywhere | Nothing. If a status needs marking, use `.dot.ok / .warn / .risk` |
| Icon fonts, icon sets, inline decorative SVG icons | Nothing. The type carries the meaning |
| Gradients other than the two already in the stylesheet | Flat surfaces |
| `border-radius` above 6px | 4px on cards, 6px on numbered cards. That is the whole vocabulary |
| Font weights above 600 for display type | 300 for anything large. Light is the signature |
| Centred body text | Left aligned, ragged right |
| Drop shadows beyond the defined `--shadow` | The barely-there one |
| More than one accent colour | One red. `--accent` for large, `--accent-text` for small |
| Colour as decoration | Colour only where it means something |
| Full-width scrolling layout | Fixed A4 pages |

### Language

Banned words and phrases, because they are the vocabulary of filler:

*Key Insights · Overview · Summary (as a heading on its own) · Deep Dive · Leverage
(as a verb) · Unlock · Game-changing · Delve · Seamless · Robust (unqualified) ·
Best-in-class · Generated by AI · Powered by AI*

Also banned:

- **Headings that name a topic instead of stating a finding.** *"Delivery Status"* tells
  the reader nothing. *"Eight active initiatives, four fully on track"* is the finding.
- **Bullet lists of exactly three** where prose would do. The reflex to produce three of
  everything is one of the loudest tells.
- **Restating a card's label in its own caption.** If the card says Total Revenue, the
  caption must add something, not repeat.
- **Hedging.** *"There may be some potential delays"* is noise. *"Legal review has run
  eleven weeks"* is information.

## Why the specific choices

Understanding the reasoning matters more than memorising the list, because it lets you
make the right call in a case the list does not cover.

**Warm paper, not white.** `#F7F3EC` is the colour of a printed report, not a web app.
It signals *document* before a single word is read, and it is easier on the eye across
nine pages than pure white.

**One red, rationed.** A single accent used sparingly makes the eye go exactly where you
want. The same red used on every card makes the eye go nowhere. Red appears on: figures
inside the hero sentence, captions that carry risk, the emphasis group, and the numbered
badges. Nowhere else.

**Light weight at large sizes.** A 40px headline at weight 300 reads as calm and
expensive. The same headline at 700 reads as a marketing banner. Light type at scale is
the single most effective signal that a designer was involved — and it is the choice a
generic generator never makes, because bold feels safer.

**4px radius.** Large radii read as *app*. Small radii read as *print*. The difference
between 4px and 14px is the difference between a report and a landing page.

**Left aligned, ragged right.** Centred text is for invitations. Every serious document
— annual report, prospectus, broadsheet — is left aligned, because the eye needs a
consistent left edge to return to.

**Two reds, not one.** `--accent` (`#C1483C`) is for large text and graphics, where WCAG
requires 3:1. `--accent-text` (`#A93B30`) is for small captions, where 4.5:1 is required.
The lighter red measures 4.45:1 on warm paper — a fail at small sizes. This is exactly
the kind of detail that separates a real design system from a colour palette.

**Fixed pages, not a scroll.** An executive report gets printed, or read as a PDF, or
projected. A scrolling page has no page numbers, no consistent unit of attention, and no
way to say "see page 4".

## Emphasis discipline

At most **one** `.is-emphasis` and **one** `.risk-callout` per page.

This is the rule most often broken, and breaking it is fatal. The purpose of emphasis is
contrast. Two emphasised things on a page halve the effect; four eliminate it entirely
and the page reverts to looking like a dashboard, where everything is equally loud and
therefore nothing is heard.

If a page genuinely has two urgent things, that is a signal the content needs splitting
across two pages, not that the rule needs bending.

---

## The test

Show the output to someone who did not commission it and ask: *"who made this?"*

- If the answer is *"looks like AI"* — something in the list above survived.
- If the answer is *"someone in marketing built a template"* — correct.
- If they do not think to ask — better still.
